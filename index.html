The product

One record for the
plant, not another inbox

Manufacturers already collect machine and sensor data. What is missing is a single place to turn those signals into tracked alerts, maintenance actions, and reviewed model findings.

Veldren keeps facilities, production lines, and assets together with the signal summaries, anomaly alerts, work orders, and prediction history that belong to them.

How it fits

Architecture for a
single plant record

See the stack
01

Registry

Facilities, lines, and assets are the spine. Signals, alerts, orders, and model outputs attach to that asset instead of a separate spreadsheet.

02

Signal and alert layer

Telemetry summaries land on the asset. Anomaly alerts stay in a review queue until a reliability engineer accepts, dismisses, or turns them into work.

03

Action and model review

Work orders close the loop. Predictive-maintenance outputs and prediction history stay on the asset so a finding can be checked later.

  1. 01

    Onsite inference

    Proposed architecture for running predictive maintenance models closer to the plant and returning outputs for review.

  2. 02

    Signal store

    Summaries of sensor and telemetry records, kept against the asset so history is available without a second system.

  3. 03

    Model outputs

    Prediction records and model findings stored per asset, ready for a structured review rather than an unread export.

  4. 04

    Human review

    Engineers accept or reject a model finding and, when it holds, open the maintenance work order from the same screen.

Where it runs

Manufacturing use cases

Same plant record for teams that run different processes, with maintenance practice that does not change from one facility to the next.

Discrete plants

Automotive and electronics lines where a press, robot, or test station needs its own alert and work-order history.

Where it runs

Each station keeps its own history

A press, robot, or test station is its own asset. The alert and the work order that follow stay on that record, so the line does not split the story across tools.

  • The station sits on the plant registry with its own signal summary.
  • An anomaly stays on that asset until a reviewer accepts it.
  • Maintenance opens a work order still linked to the same asset and the original signal.

Process plants

Food, beverage, and chemical operations where unreviewed anomalies turn into unplanned stops.

Where it runs

An anomaly stays visible before it becomes a stop

Food, beverage, and chemical operations use the same plant record. An unreviewed anomaly remains on the asset instead of turning into an unplanned stop with no history.

  • Vessels, lines, and pumps keep a signal summary on the asset.
  • The anomaly stays open until someone accepts it or sets it aside.
  • A work order opened from that review stays tied to the signal that raised it.

Equipment operators

Teams who run the assets and need health, open alerts, and the order already assigned before the shift starts.

Where it runs

The shift starts from the asset

Operators need health, open alerts, and the work order already assigned before the shift starts. Those three sit on the same record.

  • Asset health is on the registry before the crew starts.
  • Open alerts wait on the asset, so the shift does not hunt through another queue.
  • The work order already assigned is on that asset at the start of the shift.
Request a walkthrough

Maintenance providers

Service teams who cover more than one site and need the same path from alert to work order at each facility.

Where it runs

The same path at every site

Service teams who cover more than one facility use one maintenance practice. The path does not change from plant to plant.

  • Alert, review, and work order follow the same steps at each site.
  • The order stays linked to the asset and the signal that started it.
  • A provider picks up the record at any facility without rebuilding the workflow.
